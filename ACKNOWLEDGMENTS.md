# Acknowledgments

To my mom, who gave me my first NES when I was around five years old.

Looking back now, I realize that buying me that video game console wasn't easy for you. I truly appreciate it. Thanks, Mom. This one's for you.

I didn't know it at the time, but that gray box would change everything. I remember booting up Super Mario Bros for the first time—the blue sky, the bouncing Goombas, the satisfying *ding* of collecting coins. I was hooked instantly. That little plumber jumping through World 1-1 sparked something in me that never went away.

Somewhere between dying to the first Koopa and finally reaching the flagpole, I made a quiet promise to myself: *When I grow up, I want to make these.*

I did become a programmer. But life takes you on different paths—I didn't learn 6502 assembly as my first language, and I didn't start by making NES games. I learned other languages, built other things, solved other problems. But that promise I made to myself as a kid? It never went away. It just waited.

Learning to program for the NES wasn't easy. It took me several attempts. My first real try was during the pandemic—I had a hard time wrapping my head around assembly language and the NES hardware. Then a random Google search led me to [Famicom Party](https://famicom.party/), a work-in-progress book about making NES games in assembly by **Kevin Zurawel**. At the time, it only had a few chapters, but they were enough to help me take my first baby steps. Thank you, Kevin—your book gave me the foundation I needed.

The years that followed were full of attempts. I kept programming, kept failing, and couldn't seem to get a game across the finish line. Then came 2026, and with it, the AI boom. With the help of [Claude Code](https://claude.ai/code) by **Anthropic**, I finally did it. Thank you to everyone at Anthropic who built the tools that made this possible.

To my partner **Nancy**, who isn't a huge fan of video games—thank you. Watching you play, seeing you get excited on a good run and frustrated when you lost, that engagement meant everything. It pushed me to finish what I started.

To **Dong Nguyen**, creator of Flappy Bird—thank you. Choosing Flappy Bird as my first game kept the scope achievable. Your game inspired millions, and now it's inspired this little NES tribute too.

And finally, to the engineers, artists, and dreamers at **Nintendo** who built the Famicom and NES—thank you for creating the machine that changed my life.

Decades later, here I am—writing 6502 assembly, crafting sprites pixel by pixel, and building a game for the very console that started it all. The five-year-old me would be proud.
